# Tool guide for the updates writer

This guide is read by the AI that drafts process and product updates inside the tool. Add a section here whenever a feature is built or changed. Write plain facts only: what the feature is, who uses it, what people do in it, what the rules are. Do not put secrets, keys or links here.

## Navigation and roles
Admins and team leads see: Live Dashboard, Team and Access, Breaks and Roster, Calls and Summary, Bonus Guide, Tickets and Lifecycle, Transfer review (shown as Ticket audits), Alerts, Assessments, Settings, AI Agent and Debug.
Agents see: Dashboard, Break Bot, Bonus Guide, AI Writer, Log Ticket, My Ticket Stats, Ticket alerts, Transfer review, Updates, Assessments and Hall of Fame.
Nobody can sign in until an admin approves their access request in Team and Access, Access Control. Only @adit.com Google accounts can request access.

## Live Dashboard and productivity
Shows the T1 team live from RingCentral: who is available, on a call or on a break, call counts, queue waits and missed or abandoned calls. Agents have their own dashboard with their numbers for the day. Times use CST.

## Breaks, Break Bot and regularise requests
Agents record login, logout, break, BRB, training, QA and internal call events with Break Bot. Break events feed the break reports and Google Chat posts. An agent who forgot a tap can send a regularise request for the missing event and time. Any admin approves or declines it. An approved request adds the event to that day marked Regularised and does not post to Google Chat.

## Roster and schedule
The Roster page replaces the T1 CS Team Roster spreadsheet. Each agent has a daily code such as present, off, PL, UPL, SL, holiday, WFH, on duty or NCNS. Every change is kept in a history log. Schedules can be versioned and adherence (late, early, break overage) is measured against them.

## Alerts and Google Chat reports
Alerts post to Google Chat spaces and also show on the Alerts pages. Every live alert, including review alerts and escalation alerts, only posts between 7 AM and 7 PM Central time, every day. The one exception is Break log alerts, which post between 6 AM and 8 PM Central time. Anything still open is posted when the window opens the next morning. Each alert tile on the Alerts page has a Timing section: live ops has repeat every for queue waits and for nobody available plus how often tickets are checked, and review alerts have first alert, repeat every and stop after. Live ops alerts cover long queue waits, no coverage (nobody Available while agents are logged in), unassigned tickets and tickets assigned with no action. Chat Wing agents take chats, so they do not count toward queue call coverage. Productivity and break reports can be posted to Google Chat now or on a daily, weekly or monthly schedule. Reports use the same numbers as the dashboards.

## Ticket alerts for agents
Agents see unassigned tickets and their own tickets that are waiting for action, so they can act before a lead has to chase them.

## Tickets and lifecycle
Ticket data syncs from Zoho Desk in the background: status, channel, classification, category, module, FCR and ownership changes. Agents can log a ticket with Log Ticket and see their own numbers in My Ticket Stats. Chat counts and response times come from Zoho SalesIQ. CSAT comes from Zoho Analytics.

The department hand-off draft fills Client Name, Practice Name, Account Number (the CRM Acct Number, the short one), Deal Stage (OB, CSM or Churn from the CRM stage), Callback Number and Email from the ticket's CRM contact in AditKB, and uses the conversation only for the reason and resolution. Review alerts bot avatar: the image at /review-bot-avatar.png on the app can be used as the Avatar URL when creating the Google Chat webhook.

Review settings live on their own admin-only tab (Transfer review, Settings): review window and reminders, the two webhooks, direct assign teams (filterable chips), department chat spaces (one row per team), and the people directory, with a save bar that shows unsaved changes. Webhooks: Review alerts is for idle tickets (falls back to the Live ops webhook), Reviewer messages is a separate space for the messages reviewers send to agents; both can be set here or on the Alerts page. Reviewer messages can be turned off with the switch on the Alerts page. Moved tickets (verdict missing) show as a compact list, one line per ticket with ticket, agent, channel, status, who it moved to and the decision buttons; use the search box or the agent chips (with counts) to narrow a long list, and click a row or Deal history to open the deal panel in place. The Review sidebar entry shows the number of tickets waiting as a red badge on the corner of its tile. All Transfer review pages use the full page width: pending and moved cards flow into columns on wide screens, and the Settings tab shows its cards in two columns. Deal history opens smoothly inside the card: a row of summary numbers (tickets, open, on hold, closed, last 14 days, FCR, CSAT), then the support journey and issue history on the left with unsolved queries, usual modules and the agents who worked the deal on the right. The journey chart keeps a fixed, compact height, spreads tickets from the same day so they do not overlap, and shows ticket details on hover. The written account analysis now comes from AditKB's summarised analysis endpoint (the raw analysis row is close to 1 MB, which is why it often failed). It fills account health, the analysis summary, issue history with each issue's status and how often it was raised, and up to five high severity findings. When an account has no analysis, the panel says so and falls back to themes read from the ticket subjects. AditKB keys: every read tries ADITKB_ESC_API_KEY first; when AditKB refuses a table or endpoint for that key (403), the other keys set in Railway (ADITKB_ACTIVITY_API_KEY, ADITKB_API_KEY, ADITKB_CALLS_API_KEY) are tried and the one that works is remembered for that table. The journey chart tooltip now opens below the mark when the mark is in the top lane so it is never cut off. When a deal is escalated, the escalation status is followed by the escalation owner (from the active escalation in CRM, with the date it started). When a deal was de-escalated earlier, it shows the last escalation owner with the de-escalation date.

Client lookup (agents, the Tickets and Alerts page, second tab next to Ticket alerts; the sidebar tile reads Tkt Alerts): type a ticket number, deal name, account name, phone number or email and pick the client. The page shows the account and deal strip (stage, escalation and its owner, CSM, OB), office details, the people on the account, other deals, and the same support history panel as Deal history. A box asks what the client is calling about (or tap a topic such as Phone issue). It lists earlier tickets on that topic, the known issues and serious findings that match, and the latest messages of matching tickets (calls show as Call), and the Show me button adds AI guidance: what to say first, what to check, what you can do now and when to escalate. Without AI it falls back to general guidance for the topic. The page is laid out as an account header (stage, escalation and owner, CSM, OB, office details, open tickets, last ticket), then the call box beside a rail with the people on the account (phone and email are links, five shown, Show all for the rest) and the deals on the account, then the full support history with the journey chart, issue history in columns and the smaller panels in a row. Long analysis summaries show a few lines with Show the full summary. Every agent must finish a short guided tour of Client lookup once, the first time they sign in after this release (and an admin the first time they switch to agent view): it cannot be closed or skipped, each step pauses briefly, and the last step needs a tick before Finish. Completion is saved per person (table tool_tours). When the search is a ticket number, the page also reads that ticket (conversation, status, escalation) and suggests the best next step. Email and web tickets always get an email reply draft (copy it, edit it, send it from Zoho Desk), with a Get a call guide button only when the client asks for a call. Phone tickets get a call guide (what to start with, what to cover, what to ask, what not to say) with an email follow-up on request. The suggestion also shows what to check first, what the agent can do now and when to escalate. It leans on similar solved tickets and escalation cases from AditKB's case index (what finally resolved them) and on recent process and product updates, and lists which ones it used under Based on. While a search or a client is loading, a card shows what is being read (finding the account, reading tickets, checking escalations) with a moving progress bar, so a slow lookup never looks stuck. The tour has a step on ticket numbers (email reply draft for email and web, call guide for phone, other one on request). The ticket next-step feature is also posted as its own in-tool update so agents who already finished the tour hear about it (the tour is not repeated). The release is also posted once as an in-tool update (Updates page, bell notice and the must-read screen at sign-in). Search and history come from AditKB, so a very new ticket may not appear yet.

Transfer review, required fields and rework rounds: a verdict can no longer be saved with blanks (checked in the form and again on the server). Approved needs a comment of at least 5 characters (what was checked and where the ticket went) and the person it was moved to; Needs rework needs the severity (Fatal or Feedback) and a comment, and its destination is filled in automatically as the agent who handled it (T1); Skip needs a reason. The fields show Required and turn red when missing. When a ticket is sent for rework and comes back to review, it is stacked as one line: in History there is one row per ticket with a Reworked N times dropdown listing every round (verdict, reviewer, destination, time, comment, oldest first), and the pending and moved cards show Came back after rework with a collapsed list of the earlier rounds. History filters apply to each ticket's latest matching round, and the count is by ticket.

Refresh loading screen: while the page boots after a refresh, public/loading-anim.css shows the Adit wordmark filling in with colour from left to right over a faint ghost copy (public/adit-logo-color.png and adit-logo-ghost-light/dark.png) with a slim progress bar at the top, and keeps the footer hidden so it never sits at the top of an empty page. public/loading-anim.js marks the app as ready once the page has loaded and the first requests have settled (about half a second, never more than 6 seconds), then the screen fades out. Reduced-motion users get the same screen without movement.

Verdict form: the save button (Save as approved, Save as fatal, Save as feedback, Skip) stays faded and disabled until everything the verdict needs is filled in: a comment of at least 5 characters, plus who it moved to for Approved, or Fatal/Feedback for Needs rework. The Message to the agent box now shows only for Needs rework; on Approved the reviewer takes the follow-up action themselves, so no message is drafted.

Own tickets: a reviewer can review a ticket they handled themselves in Transfer review. The old block (a 403 'another reviewer has to decide' on saving and on sending the agent message, and no Approved/Needs rework buttons on the reviewer's own ticket) is removed; the card just says 'Your own ticket, you can review it'. SPOC ticket audits (a separate feature) still keep their own rule.

Disk watch: every 30 minutes (and a minute after start) the server measures how full the data volume is. At 80% and again at 90% it posts one notice a day to admins in the notification centre, naming the largest tables, so the volume is topped up before it fills and writes fail with SQLITE_FULL (agents then see 'database or disk is full' and 'Authentication required', because sessions cannot be saved). The admin-only /api/db-stats now also returns volume (used, free, percent) and biggest (largest tables in MB). Nothing is deleted by this; the 2-hourly prune still only covers the older core tables, not assessment photos or ticket history.

Data housekeeping: assessment camera photos now default to 14 days (the owner can still choose 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 or 180 in Assessments, Access; a value already saved there is kept). At start and every 2 hours the server also clears desk_ticket_events (legacy, unused) after 30 days, empties the bulky detail_text of ticket history rows after 30 days (the rows, counts and destinations stay), and clears cached read-aloud audio after 60 days (it is regenerated on the next play). Ticket lifecycle tables are a mirror of AditKB and Zoho Desk, so they are not archived to Drive; the thin snapshot, agent and survey rows are kept. Disk watch (80 and 90 percent notices to admins) tells you if more is needed.

Emergency space: at start-up, before anything writes, lib/emergency-space.js checks the volume. At 95% or more (and less than 60 MB of free pages inside the database file) it keeps SQLite's journal in memory so deleting needs no free disk, deletes cached read-aloud audio, the legacy ticket event cache, then assessment camera photos older than 7 days (oldest first) in small batches until about 60 MB of pages are free, and restores the journal mode. The file does not shrink, so Railway's graph stays at 100%, but the freed pages are reused, so sessions and sign-ins can write again. Disk watch now subtracts those free pages (livePct) so it does not nag while there is room inside the file. If the app is down at 100%, redeploying runs this again.

Ticket copy keep time: the app keeps a copy of Zoho Desk tickets only for the last 90 days (created date). Older ticket copies are deleted only by the emergency routine at start-up when the storage is nearly full (95% or more); the routine 2-hourly trim is paused so no more history is removed. AditKB still holds every original ticket. Reports that look further back than 90 days will show fewer tickets.

Full-disk mode: when the Railway volume is full, SQLite cannot even create its small rollback journal file, so every save fails even if there are free pages inside the database. At start-up the app now keeps that journal in memory for as long as the volume is nearly full, which lets sign-ins and syncs save again. The database file itself does not shrink, so the Railway graph can stay near 100% while the app works normally. The trim of old tickets runs in the background at start-up so it never delays the rest of the app, and the automatic compaction (VACUUM) is skipped while full-disk mode is on because an interrupted rewrite without a disk journal could damage the file.

Older ticket history: the volume keeps full ticket detail from 12 July 2026 onward. When a ticket report, an agent's My Stats, a ticket list or Verify ticket uses a date range that starts earlier, the app loads those months from AditKB into a cache on the server's own scratch disk (not the 500 MB volume) and works the numbers out with the same rules. The first look at an older month takes a few seconds per month; after that it is instant until the next restart. Two things only Zoho's live API provides are not loaded for old months: field-change history (tickets updated, transfers read from history) and Zoho Analytics FCR flags (the older FCR rule is used). AditKB never hands out customer contact details in bulk, so for old months the customer search matches the account name only (not contact name or email). Months load one at a time. The cache is only ever placed on a disk other than the volume (if none exists it is switched off), and every 30 minutes the log shows one storage line (volume use, database size, free pages, cache size). CSAT survey rows are kept for all time. Tickets with a transfer review or an audit are copied back onto the volume whatever their age, and adding an old ticket to audits by number copies it from AditKB first.

Review page speed: the account and deal details on the waiting tiles are looked up three at a time within one 3-second budget for the whole page. If AditKB is busy, the page opens anyway and the missing details fill in when a tile is opened (before, each tile could wait 3 seconds in turn, so a busy AditKB kept the page loading for over half a minute).

Support guides: a Guides page (sidebar, agent and admin views, also at /guides) with three tabs. VoIP support is the T1 CS VoIP Support Guide and Query Catalog with a contents list and search that highlights every match. AI support is the T1 AI Support Runbook, copied from t1-ai-support.web.app and shown with its own design. Ask AI answers a question from these two guides only: it picks the closest passages, writes a short answer and links to the sections it used, and says so when the guides do not cover it. Agents see it like any other agent page (it can be switched off under Agent pages). People outside the team get in through Access Control > Support guides guests (or by approving an access request as "Guest (support guides only)"): they sign in with Google and see only /guides. To refresh a guide, the new Word file or web page is re-imported into content/guides/. The VoIP guide is laid out like the AI runbook (title block, part banners, numbered sections, one card per topic, "Who handles it" lines as green callouts); this is layout only, the guide's text is unchanged word for word. The three tabs are built once and then only hidden or shown, so switching is instant, the AI runbook does not reload and each tab keeps its scroll position; guide cards off screen are not drawn until you scroll near them, and search highlights matches without rebuilding the page. Every Zoho Desk ticket number in the VoIP guide (and in Ask AI answers) is a link: /guides/ticket/<number> finds the ticket in the app's own ticket copy or in AditKB and opens it in Zoho Desk. On import (12 Oct 2026) all 990 ticket numbers in the VoIP guide were checked in AditKB: all exist and were created between 15 Apr and 10 Oct 2026; 966 sit in VoIP teams, 23 have no team but are owned by Phones agents, and one (409238, a roles and permissions case) belongs to T2 Core.

Escalated clients and stale reviews: Escalation watch now lists every T1 ticket from a client who already has an active escalation in CRM (matched by account id, deal id or account name), even when no message trips a keyword or the AI. Use Scan now with a longer look-back to pick up tickets from the last few days. Live state on the Break board, Chat Wing duty and queue misses: each Break board card shows, every 20 seconds, whether a Call Wing agent is on a call (with direction and call time) or not, and how many chats a Chat Wing agent has open in SalesIQ (read from SalesIQ, at most 45 seconds old, from GET /api/live-floor/now). Chat Wing agents are not expected to be available on the phone, so on the live dashboard they show as Chat duty instead of Unavailable and are left out of the unavailable counts and the Needs attention alerts. Missed today is now the queue's own count, abandoned plus voicemail, like RingCentral Live Reports, with the split shown under the label, and Team right now also shows Voicemail today. An inbound queue call that nobody answered and that did not go to voicemail now counts as abandoned whatever RingCentral's result says (for example Hang Up), and the server log line for the queue summary lists the results it counted as abandoned.

Queue numbers from the queue's own call log: Inbound, Abandoned and Voicemail for today now come from the RingCentral call queue's own call log, the same source RingCentral Live Reports uses for a queue. Every inbound call the queue received counts; Accepted or Call connected is answered, Voicemail is voicemail, and anything else (Missed, Hang Up and so on) is abandoned. Queue today on the admin dashboard names the queue the numbers come from, and the server log line for the queue summary shows the count of each result.

AI & VoIP support dashboard (Tickets, AI & VoIP): T1 took over basic AI support on 8 Sep 2026 and basic VoIP support on 12 Oct 2026. For each topic the page counts, from the Desk tickets synced from AditKB, the tickets T1 handled, how many T1 resolved, how many are still with T1, how many T1 transferred (and to which team), and how many went straight to the team without reaching T1. A ticket belongs to a topic when it went through that topic's team (T2 - AI Support; any VoIP or Porting team) or its Desk topic or subject is about it. Calls and chats are the Phone and Chat tickets T1 logs. It shows a channel split, a daily kept versus transferred chart, the two weeks before T1 took over against now, a per-agent table, topics and transfer destinations, and a ticket list. Audit asks AI, with the support guide and the ticket conversation from AditKB, whether T1 could have handled a transferred ticket; verdicts are stored (table topic_audits) and counted per agent. API: GET /api/topic-support, POST /api/topic-support/audit (admins only).

AI & VoIP dashboard reads AditKB live: the page now reads the tickets straight from AditKB (non-personal columns only), so today's tickets are included; if AditKB is busy it falls back to the copy synced into the tool and says so. VoIP teams are now T1 - VoIP CSM, T1 - VoIP OB, T2 - VoIP BOPS and T2 - VoIP Assets; T2 - Porting (SMS, 10DLC, number ports) and T3 - VoIP are no longer counted as VoIP support.

AI & VoIP dashboard sorts tickets against the support guides: each AI or VoIP candidate ticket (subject, Desk topic, team and the start of the description) is sorted by AI into a guide area, such as VoIP call flow and DND, eFax, phones offline, audio, caller ID, or AI booking and EHR sync, SMS Agent, Call Intelligence, cancellations, with who the guide says should handle it: T1 resolves, T1 checks first, owning team, or AI leads only. The areas and owners come from the AI runbook's routing map, Who owns what and T1 authority, and the VoIP guide's first-call requests and When to hand over. Each ticket is sorted once and stored (table topic_scope); new tickets are sorted in the background and the page refreshes itself. A new tile, Against the guide, counts tickets T1 transferred although the guide says T1 resolves them, plus commercial AI requests kept by T1; the By guide area table and an Against the guide filter show them. Tickets with a vague subject (for example a chat titled only with the client's issue) are now found through their description.

Call backs are VoIP's: T1 CS cannot make outbound calls, so when a client needs a call back (for example they went unresponsive in chat before the fix was done, or asked to be called), sending the ticket to VoIP is a correct transfer. The guide sorting and the transfer audit both know this rule, and a transfer an audit finds right is not counted in Against the guide.

AI & VoIP ticket reviews and reports: every AI or VoIP ticket a T1 agent resolved or transferred is reviewed in the background (every 30 minutes, up to 12 at a time). The review reads the whole ticket in Zoho Desk: the description, every reply and note, the SalesIQ chat transcript, matching T1 call transcripts around the ticket time, and the owner history, together with the support guide, and rates the T1 handling 1 to 5 on guide adherence, resolution, communication and hand-off (hand-off only when transferred), with an overall rating, a summary, what was done well, coaching points and anything missing from the hand-off (table topic_reviews). Each ticket row has a rating that opens the review, and Review or Review again. Agent scorecards show each agent's averages, weakest area, top coaching points and lowest rated tickets. Export to Excel downloads every ticket in the range with outcome, guide area, ratings and coaching points (CSV). A weekly report for the previous Monday to Sunday (Central) is built automatically with trends and coaching points, shown under Weekly reports and announced in the bell (Build last week's report now rebuilds it). The page now reads every ticket a T1 agent touched in the range, not only ones whose subject looks like AI or VoIP, and sorts them against the guide; searching a ticket number that is not listed offers Check why. The page says Zoho, not the data service name. APIs: POST /api/topic-support/review, GET /api/topic-support/export, GET /api/topic-support/why, GET and POST /api/topic-support/weekly(/run).

AI & VoIP mishandling checks: every AI and VoIP ticket is checked against the guides and its review. Flags: transferred though the guide says T1 resolves it (unless an audit or review found the transfer right, for example a call back); a cancellation, downgrade, upgrade or minutes request kept by T1; sent to the wrong team (AI booking or EHR sync straight to AI Support instead of the Online Scheduling SOP, missing recordings to AI Support instead of VoIP Support, Automated Reminders or Forms sent to the AI team, Call Intelligence not sent to AI Support); a VoIP issue sent outside VoIP; the review found the transfer was not needed; rated 2 or lower on overall, guide or resolution; a weak hand-off note; still with T1 after 24 hours. The page shows a Needs attention tile and card, a filter, and a red label on each flagged ticket; the export has a Needs attention column. Every 10 minutes the server reviews new tickets (newest first) and sends a bell alert to admins and active SPOCs for each newly flagged ticket and problem (urgent for wrong team and commercial requests); the first run sends one summary instead (table topic_alerts).

AI & VoIP counts: Zoho's own Desk topic now decides first. A ticket Zoho files under VoIP & Phone Issues, Phone hardware, Fax or an AI topic always counts for that topic, and so does a ticket whose subject clearly says so (for example "issues with the phone"). The guide sorting only decides the rest. "New Call from ... - 1025" emails are voicemail notices from our own support line, so they no longer count as VoIP unless the sorting finds a phone problem in them. Every ticket is sorted again once after this change. Check why now says which rule counted a ticket.

AI & VoIP shows only T1 work: the page now counts only calls, chats and tickets a T1 agent handled whose topic, by the guides, is AI or VoIP. Tickets that went straight to the AI or VoIP team without reaching T1 (for example #415487) are left out, and the Went straight to team tile and filter are gone.

AI & VoIP test tickets: each ticket row has Test ticket, leave out. It takes that ticket out of every count, rating, alert and the weekly report. Left-out tickets are listed under the ticket list with Count it again, and Check why says when a ticket was left out.

AI & VoIP reads the whole ticket: every ticket that could count is now read in full (client messages, agent replies, internal notes and comments, chat transcripts). Test tickets, blank or silent calls, wrong numbers, spam and duplicates are left out automatically (for example #415523, where the note says it was a blank call posted as a test ticket), and the reading also decides whether the ticket is really AI or VoIP; Zoho's own AI or VoIP topic still counts. A ticket is read again when it changes in Zoho. Left-out tickets show under the list with the reason and Count it again.

AI & VoIP over time: the By day chart is now Over time, with Day, Week, Month and Quarter. Each bar shows its total, and pointing at a bar (or tabbing to it) shows the breakdown: handled, resolved by T1, still with T1, transferred with percentages, and calls, chats and tickets. Periods with nothing show as empty gaps.

AI & VoIP test ticket fix: a note that plainly says test ticket, blank call, wrong number, spam call or similar now leaves the ticket out straight away, without waiting for AI. The AI reading uses short ticket keys so it cannot mix up long Zoho ids, every ticket is read again once, and Check why reads a ticket on the spot if it has not been read yet.

AI & VoIP start dates: AI support counts from 8 Sep 2026 (corrected from 1 Oct) and VoIP from 12 Oct 2026. The range button is now Since go-live.

Client lookup open tickets and redraft: Support history now includes the account's tickets that Zoho never linked to a deal (app alerts, emails), so open tickets like #382804 show. ## Transfer review and the 5 strike policy
T1 agents do not move tickets to other teams or people themselves. They set the ticket status to the review status (default name Pending Review - T1). A reviewer (SPOC) checks it within the review window, moves it to the right person in Zoho Desk and records a verdict in the tool.
Verdicts: Approved, Needs rework or Skip (Skip is only for tickets that already left the status, for example set by mistake). An invalid transfer is marked Fatal or Feedback. Fatal counts as a strike for the agent. Feedback is not a strike but the agent sees it. Strikes stay active for 90 days and a reviewer or admin can remove one.
Teams in the assign directly list skip review. If a T1 agent moves a ticket to any other team without the review status it is listed as Skipped review for a reviewer to decide, and it is never a strike on its own.
The review window is counted only between the review hours set in Review settings (default 7 AM to 7 PM Central time). If a ticket waits past the review window inside those hours, the people set in settings are tagged in Google Chat. Nobody is tagged outside review hours, and the clock resumes at the start of the next day's hours.
Each waiting ticket, and each ticket under Moved, verdict missing, shows a deal card (account and deal name on top, then stage, escalation, CSM and OB as chips, with a coloured edge for the escalation state) with the Deal history toggle and the Approved, Needs rework and Skip buttons on one row. It carries these fields: account name, deal name, deal stage, CSM, onboarding (OB) owner and escalation status. Clicking the ticket opens the deal history for the reviewer: issue history (with the account health), unsolved queries, modules usually reported, earlier transfer reviews on the same deal, a flag when three or more tickets came in within 14 days, the support journey (a timeline with open, on hold and closed tickets, labelled only where still live, and a dropdown with the full ticket list; clicking a mark opens that ticket in Zoho Desk), the agents who worked tickets, and FCR and CSAT for that deal. Issue history, satisfaction and modules come from Adit's written account analysis, and the ticket list, FCR and CSAT are live. If Adit's written analysis cannot be read, issue history and modules are derived from the account's own tickets (grouped by theme, for example repeated EHR disconnections, with a likely cause such as the Adit server or the EHR bridge). Stage and escalation status are colour coded, CSAT appears once, and an opened panel stays open when the list refreshes.
Review alerts can be tuned in Review settings or on the Review alerts tile in Alerts: first alert after a number of minutes, repeat every number of minutes, stop after a number of reminders (0 means no limit), and on or off. The tagged people are set in Review settings. These alerts post to the live ops space and never outside alert hours.
When a reviewer picks Approved, the tool drafts the hand-off message for the receiving department's Google Chat space (client name, practice, account number, deal stage, callback, email, ticket link, reason for contact, resolution). The reviewer checks it, copies it and posts it in the space. The tool never posts it. Admins save each department's space link in Review settings.
When a reviewer picks Needs rework, the tool suggests feedback based on earlier reviewer comments. Agents see tips built from their own feedback in Transfer policy. History lists every review and can be searched, so a later escalation can be traced back to its review.
The 5 strike policy: strikes 1 to 4 are a notice with the reviewer's comments, strike 5 is a verbal warning, strike 6 a written warning, strike 7 a performance improvement plan for 30 days, strike 8 and more disciplinary action.

## Escalation watch
Watches T1 tickets and calls for clients who may need an escalation and posts an alert to a Google Chat space through a webhook. Detection is keyword first: cancel, cancellation, port out, switching provider, leaving Adit, wanting past invoices, being unhappy for a long time and similar. The admin can edit the keyword list. AI reads the ticket or call to confirm and write a short summary, but AI alone needs a minimum confidence. Call confidence on its own is not enough.
If the client has no escalation on record, the alert asks the team to create one in CRM. If the client already has an escalation, no new-escalation alert is posted; the tool checks whether the agent tagged the right escalation owner in the ticket or assigned it to the right person, and flags it if not. Reviewers can mark an alert as not needed with a reason (the AI learns from these) or record what action was taken and whether an escalation was created. Alerts can be filtered and searched. Reminders repeat until someone acts.

Shows the T1 team live from RingCentral: who is available, on a call or on a break, call counts, queue waits and missed or abandoned calls. Agents have their own dashboard with their numbers for the day. Times use CST.

## Breaks, Break Bot and regularise requests
Agents record login, logout, break, BRB, training, QA and internal call events with Break Bot. Break events feed the break reports and Google Chat posts. An agent who forgot a tap can send a regularise request for the missing event and time. Any admin approves or declines it. An approved request adds the event to that day marked Regularised and does not post to Google Chat.

## Roster and schedule
The Roster page replaces the T1 CS Team Roster spreadsheet. Each agent has a daily code such as present, off, PL, UPL, SL, holiday, WFH, on duty or NCNS. Every change is kept in a history log. Schedules can be versioned and adherence (late, early, break overage) is measured against them.

## Alerts and Google Chat reports
Alerts post to Google Chat spaces and also show on the Alerts pages. Every live alert, including review alerts and escalation alerts, only posts between 7 AM and 7 PM Central time, every day. The one exception is Break log alerts, which post between 6 AM and 8 PM Central time. Anything still open is posted when the window opens the next morning. Each alert tile on the Alerts page has a Timing section: live ops has repeat every for queue waits and for nobody available plus how often tickets are checked, and review alerts have first alert, repeat every and stop after. Live ops alerts cover long queue waits, no coverage (nobody Available while agents are logged in), unassigned tickets and tickets assigned with no action. Chat Wing agents take chats, so they do not count toward queue call coverage. Productivity and break reports can be posted to Google Chat now or on a daily, weekly or monthly schedule. Reports use the same numbers as the dashboards.

## Ticket alerts for agents
Agents see unassigned tickets and their own tickets that are waiting for action, so they can act before a lead has to chase them.

The department hand-off draft fills Client Name, Practice Name, Account Number (the CRM Acct Number, the short one), Deal Stage (OB, CSM or Churn from the CRM stage), Callback Number and Email from the ticket's CRM contact in AditKB, and uses the conversation only for the reason and resolution. Review alerts bot avatar: the image at /review-bot-avatar.png on the app can be used as the Avatar URL when creating the Google Chat webhook.

Webhooks: Review alerts is for idle tickets (falls back to the Live ops webhook), Reviewer messages is a separate space for the messages reviewers send to agents; both can be set here or on the Alerts page. Reviewer messages can be turned off with the switch on the Alerts page. Moved tickets (verdict missing) show as a compact list, one line per ticket with ticket, agent, channel, status, who it moved to and the decision buttons; use the search box or the agent chips (with counts) to narrow a long list, and click a row or Deal history to open the deal panel in place. It fills account health, the analysis summary, issue history with each issue's status and how often it was raised, and up to five high severity findings. When an account has no analysis, the panel says so and falls back to themes read from the ticket subjects. AditKB keys: every read tries ADITKB_ESC_API_KEY first; when AditKB refuses a table or endpoint for that key (403), the other keys set in Railway (ADITKB_ACTIVITY_API_KEY, ADITKB_API_KEY, ADITKB_CALLS_API_KEY) are tried and the one that works is remembered for that table. The journey chart tooltip now opens below the mark when the mark is in the top lane so it is never cut off. When a deal is escalated, the escalation status is followed by the escalation owner (from the active escalation in CRM, with the date it started). Client lookup (agents, the Tickets and Alerts page, second tab next to Ticket alerts; the sidebar tile reads Tkt Alerts): type a ticket number, deal name, account name, phone number or email and pick the client. The page shows the account and deal strip (stage, escalation and its owner, CSM, OB), office details, the people on the account, other deals, and the same support history panel as Deal history. A box asks what the client is calling about (or tap a topic such as Phone issue). ## Transfer review and the 5 strike policy
T1 agents do not move tickets to other teams or people themselves. They set the ticket status to the review status (default name Pending Review - T1). A reviewer (SPOC) checks it within the review window, moves it to the right person in Zoho Desk and records a verdict in the tool.
Verdicts: Approved, Needs rework or Skip (Skip is only for tickets that already left the status, for example set by mistake). An invalid transfer is marked Fatal or Feedback. Fatal counts as a strike for the agent. Feedback is not a strike but the agent sees it. Strikes stay active for 90 days and a reviewer or admin can remove one.
Teams in the assign directly list skip review. If a T1 agent moves a ticket to any other team without the review status it is listed as Skipped review for a reviewer to decide, and it is never a strike on its own.
The review window is counted only between the review hours set in Review settings (default 7 AM to 7 PM Central time). If a ticket waits past the review window inside those hours, the people set in settings are tagged in Google Chat. Nobody is tagged outside review hours, and the clock resumes at the start of the next day's hours.
Each waiting ticket, and each ticket under Moved, verdict missing, shows a deal card (account and deal name on top, then stage, escalation, CSM and OB as chips, with a coloured edge for the escalation state) with the Deal history toggle and the Approved, Needs rework and Skip buttons on one row. It carries these fields: account name, deal name, deal stage, CSM, onboarding (OB) owner and escalation status. Clicking the ticket opens the deal history for the reviewer: issue history (with the account health), unsolved queries, modules usually reported, earlier transfer reviews on the same deal, a flag when three or more tickets came in within 14 days, the support journey (a timeline with open, on hold and closed tickets, labelled only where still live, and a dropdown with the full ticket list; clicking a mark opens that ticket in Zoho Desk), the agents who worked tickets, and FCR and CSAT for that deal. Issue history, satisfaction and modules come from Adit's written account analysis, and the ticket list, FCR and CSAT are live. If Adit's written analysis cannot be read, issue history and modules are derived from the account's own tickets (grouped by theme, for example repeated EHR disconnections, with a likely cause such as the Adit server or the EHR bridge). Stage and escalation status are colour coded, CSAT appears once, and an opened panel stays open when the list refreshes.
