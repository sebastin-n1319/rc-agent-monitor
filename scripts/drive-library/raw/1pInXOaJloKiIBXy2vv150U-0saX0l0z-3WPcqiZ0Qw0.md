Bank payments and Convenience Fees, for the Sales team and the Upgrades team

This is in two halves. The first half is a tour of the Adit Pay module, screen by screen, so you know what you are actually selling and what it looks like when a practice opens it. The second half is the playbook: seven specific ways to put money back into a practice, each with the number it is worth, the words to use, and the objection you will hit.

# Part One. What Adit Pay is

Adit Pay is the payments module inside Adit, on the left navigation bar. It is not a separate product with its own login, and it is not somebody else’s processor resold under our name. Payments sit in the same platform as scheduling, forms, insurance verification, reminders and the ledger, which is the whole reason it exists.

Click Adit Pay and the module opens with its own sub-navigation. Everything below is one of those items. A practice does not have to use all of it, and most use a fraction, which is where most of your opportunity lives.

## What sits inside the module

* **Payments.** Every transaction, filterable by status. The default landing screen.

* **Payment Plans.** Balances split across scheduled installments.

* **Subscriptions.** Recurring memberships and plans.

* **Disputes.** Chargebacks and their status.

* **Payouts.** Money on its way to the practice bank account.

* **Payment Reminders.** Automated chasing of unpaid requests.

* **Insights.** The reporting dashboard across all of the above.

* **Preferences.** Three sub-pages: Payment Request, Business Details and Terminals. This is where the settings that matter commercially live.

## The three rates everything else comes from

* **Card on a terminal, in the office:** 2.25% plus \$0.20 per transaction.

* **Card keyed, online or by text link:** 2.90% plus \$0.30 per transaction.

* **Bank / ACH:** 0.5% plus \$1.75 per transaction, charged to the practice and never shown to the patient.

Learn those three lines before anything else. Most of Part Two is arithmetic on the gaps between them, and the gaps are wider than practices expect.

## The part practices do not expect

The rate is the same for every client. A practice processing \$1,000 a month pays what a practice processing \$100,000 a month pays. There is no volume tier to negotiate, which sounds like a limitation until you explain why it is not: we took the rate down from our side rather than reserving the good rate for large accounts.

More importantly, the rate is the same for every card type. American Express, Discover and premium rewards cards all cost the practice exactly the same as a basic Visa debit. Adit absorbs the difference. Almost every processor a practice has used charges more for those cards, and it is usually buried in the non-qualified tier of their statement, so this lands harder than it sounds.

## And what a practice is not charged

* No minimum monthly volume, and no penalty for a quiet month.

* No contract and no cancellation fee. A practice that stops using the module simply stops paying.

* No PCI compliance fee.

* No merchant portal fee and no statement fee. Everything is visible in the module.

* No setup cost. Adit Pay is already inside the practice subscription. If they transact they pay the rate, and if they do not transact they pay nothing.

Run through that list out loud. Most of it maps directly onto line items sitting on the statement in front of them, and a practice that has never questioned those charges starts doing the arithmetic themselves.

## Payouts

Where the money actually is. Four figures: In-Transit to Bank, Future Payout, Total Balance, and the payout history. Reports download as PDF or CSV for reconciliation. Note that this screen is only visible to Manager or Owner level users, so a front desk contact may not be able to see it.

The practice chooses daily, weekly or monthly payouts. On daily, whatever has settled by end of day goes out and lands in the bank in one to two business days. On weekly they pick any day from Monday to Friday. Most choose daily, but practices that pay bills or run payroll on a cycle sometimes prefer weekly or monthly, so ask rather than assume.

One difference worth explaining before they notice it. Adit deducts the fees and sends the remainder, rather than sending the gross amount and billing the fees back the following month. That is deliberate. Billing fees in arrears requires a direct debit mandate against the practice bank account, and setting one up is a slow verification process. Deducting first means the money moves faster and nobody has to authorise a debit against their account. Practices who have been through the other model recognise the difference immediately.

# The two features that need real care

Everything above is straightforward. These two are not, and getting them wrong is how a conversation goes badly.

## Bank / ACH payments

* **It is already on.** Enabled by default for eligible US locations. A practice may be offering it without having touched a setting, which is expected rather than a bug. It can be switched off in Preferences in about a minute.

* **It costs the practice 0.5% plus \$1.75.** Carried by the practice, never shown to the patient, and it cannot be passed through.

* **The practice sends a payment method request first.** The patient links their bank through it. Only then can a payment be charged to that account.

* **Adding and verifying a bank account is free.** No fee, no hold.

* **It settles in 1 to 4 business days.** Slower than a card. Raise it before they find out, and reframe it rather than apologising for it. See below.

* **Not available in Canada.** Eligibility comes from the address on the connected payment account, assessed per location.

* **Verification is instant, through the bank login.** The patient selects their bank and signs in, and the account is verified and authorised immediately. Manual micro-deposit verification is switched off, so there is no waiting on small test deposits. Roughly 90% of banks support the instant route.

* **The authorisation is saved for future payments.** Once verified, the account can be charged again without the patient re-authorising each time, which is what makes it work for payment plans.

### *Four things that will catch you out*

* **The bank must be saved before the request.** If you send a payment request to a patient who has no saved bank account, they will not see a bank option at all. They only get cards. You have to send a payment method request first, let them link the account, and then charge it. This is the most common confusion.

* **There is no primary and secondary bank account.** Cards have a fallback: if the primary fails, Adit charges the secondary. Bank accounts do not work that way. A patient can link several, but a failed bank payment does not automatically retry against another one.

* **ACH cannot be switched off while payment plans are running on it.** The toggle will refuse and show an error. Every plan using it has to be cancelled first. That is a safety measure rather than a fault, but it surprises people, so do not promise a practice they can turn it off whenever they like without that caveat.

* **A payment in flight cannot be cancelled.** Once it has gone to the bank it runs to success or failure. Neither the practice nor the patient can pull it back mid-flight.

### *How to talk about the settlement delay*

Do not treat one to four days as a weakness to be apologised for, because the explanation is genuinely reassuring once a practice hears it.

A card payment carries a real-time authorisation check against the patient account, which is why it confirms in seconds. Bank payments have no such layer. Debits are submitted to the banking network in batches, so how long it takes depends on when in the day the payment was initiated relative to the bank cut-off. Sometimes it completes in a couple of hours. Sometimes it takes two days. This is how bank payments work everywhere in the country, not something particular to Adit, and every practice already knows it from their own banking.

Then move it from a cost to a benefit. Bank payments are an additional route, not a replacement. A patient whose card is expired, declined, damaged or simply at its limit can still pay from their account. Before this existed the practice had one way to collect electronically. Now they have two.

## Convenience Fees

* **Off until the practice turns it on.** The opposite of Bank/ACH.

* **The rate matches their processing rate.** 2.25% plus \$0.20 card-present, 2.90% plus \$0.30 card-not-present. Fixed, and a practice cannot set their own.

* **One-time card payments only.** Never on bank payments, payment plans, subscriptions, CareCredit, or HSA and FSA cards.

* **Not available in Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts or Canada.** Where a location is ineligible the option is hidden rather than greyed out, which is why some practices cannot find it at all.

* **The fee does not post to the ledger.** Only the underlying service amount posts. Fees are tracked in Adit Pay reporting. Never tell a practice the fee will appear in their ledger.

* **It can be dropped on any individual request.** Even with the setting on, the person raising a request sees a checkbox and can waive the fee for that payment. Practices like hearing this, because it means turning it on is not an all-or-nothing commitment.

* **Partial payments switch off while it is on.** Automatic and deliberate, so the fee always corresponds to one complete transaction.

* **Where it shows up.** Manual requests, text-to-pay, charging a card on file, online scheduling for new patients, and one-off treatment plan payments. The amount, the fee and the total are broken out for the patient before they pay, and again on the receipt.



*Whether the fee applies to any given payment. Check this before you promise anything*

## Is it a surcharge? No, and the distinction is legal

A surcharge can only be applied to credit cards. It is not legal on debit cards at all. A Convenience Fee applies uniformly to credit and debit alike and is disclosed to the patient as its own line item before they pay. That uniformity is exactly what puts it in a separate, compliant category. The patient also always has a fee-free route, in person or by bank where that is offered.

*No, and the distinction is a real legal one rather than just wording. A surcharge can only be charged on credit cards, it is not legal on debit at all. Convenience Fee applies the same way across both credit and debit, and it is shown to the patient before they pay. That is what keeps it in a different, compliant category.*

## The fee is grossed up, so say so first

The fee is calculated so the practice nets the billed amount after processing is deducted, which makes it slightly larger than a flat percentage on top. A \$1,000 service carries a \$30.18 fee, so the patient pays \$1,030.18 and the practice receives the full \$1,000. On \$100 it is \$102.51 in the office or \$103.30 keyed. Almost every query about a fee amount comes from a practice expecting a simple percentage, so explain it before they see it.

# Part Two. The playbook

Seven plays. Each one is a specific way to put money back into a practice, with what it is worth, who to run it on, the words, and the objection. Run the play that matches what you heard rather than the one that comes first. Most calls will surface two or three.

# Which play to run

Discovery is not a questionnaire. It is listening for one of six things and then running the matching play. Most calls will surface two or three of them.



*What you hear, and the play it points to*

# The strongest sentence you have

If a practice turns Convenience Fees on, Adit Pay costs them nothing. The processing cost goes to the patient, so a \$100 payment is \$100 in the practice bank account. There is no subscription for the module, no minimum, no PCI fee, no statement fee, no contract and no cancellation charge. They are using the payments platform for free.

Outside of Square, no other platform in our category offers a compliant convenience fee. That combination, a payments module that is free to run and a fee model almost nobody else has, is the shortest route to a yes that exists in this product.

It does not fit every practice. Some will not pass a fee to patients, and the moment they say so you drop it and move to Plays 1, 2 and 3, which all work without it. But lead with it, because when it lands the rest of the conversation becomes much easier.

# Anatomy of a call that closed

This is a real orthodontic practice, running Cloud9, processing with Global Payments, closed on a single call. The sequence is worth copying because almost nothing in it was improvised.

## It opened with their own statement, not a pitch

The rep had already run their November statement. \$25,643.58 processed, \$866.74 in total fees, which is a 3.38% effective rate. That number came from their own document, so there was nothing to argue with.

## The pivot that mattered

The rep modelled a 90/10 split favouring the terminal and showed a \$238.86 monthly saving. The doctor immediately corrected him: most of their payments are automatic charges against a card on file. The rep did not defend the model. He said "no problem, we can switch the numbers and see how it works," re-ran it live at 40/60, and showed \$147. Then again at 30/70, showing roughly \$130.

That is the most important moment in the call. The saving dropped by 45% and the deal got stronger, because the practice watched the number survive their own correction. Never defend an assumption. Re-run it in front of them.

Note what the doctor revealed without being asked: their volume is mostly recurring card-on-file, which is card not present. That single answer told the rep which plays were live and which were not.

## "Is that the best rate you can do?"

This question arrives on nearly every call. The answer given was not a negotiation, it was a reframe: the rate is identical for every client regardless of volume, and identical for every card type including American Express and premium rewards cards, which Adit absorbs. Then straight into the list of things they are not charged. No minimum, no contract, no cancellation fee, no PCI fee, no statement fee, no portal fee.

The doctor did not ask again. A practice asking for a discount is usually asking whether they are being treated fairly, and a flat rate that applies to everyone answers that better than a concession would.

## Discovery was three questions, asked mid-demo

* When you send a bill, how often does someone have to follow up, and how? The answer was calls and texts, by hand. That opened Payment Reminders.

* Do you offer any installment plan, membership or discount plan? That opened Payment Plans and Subscriptions.

* Do those payments post to the ledger automatically, or does someone post them? That opened payment posting.

None of it was a questionnaire. Each question followed naturally from what had just been demonstrated, and each answer selected the next thing to show.

## The hardest objection was integration, not price

The doctor got stuck on whether a payment plan would have to be built twice, once in Cloud9 and once in Adit. He asked four separate times in different words. It took bringing his financial coordinator onto the call and walking through the actual plan creation screen before it landed.

The answer is that the plan exists only in Adit Pay and posts each payment to the Cloud9 ledger as it processes, so the balance reduces automatically. The practice still records the treatment and balance in the PMS as they do today. Nothing is duplicated, but "nothing is duplicated" is not believable as a sentence. It becomes believable when they watch you build one.

Expect this on every practice-management integration conversation, and expect to demo rather than explain.

## The close was a small hardware decision, not a platform decision

The doctor did not agree to switch processors. He agreed to a terminal (then \$100, now \$150), a ten minute setup, and a training call. The processor switch follows from that, but it was never the thing he had to say yes to.

That is the shape to aim for. Make the first yes small, concrete and reversible.

## Two small things that did real work

* **Offering to speak to the financial coordinator immediately.** When the doctor said he wanted her opinion, the rep offered to talk to her right then rather than scheduling. She joined, asked her questions, and the objection died on the call instead of in a follow-up that might never have happened. Note the constraint though: only an owner-level user can complete setup, so you still need the decision maker back at the end.

* **A story about a real dispute.** A practice lost a \$5,000 chargeback with no signed consent form. Any payment can be disputed within 180 days with no reason given, and the practice pays a \$15 dispute fee on top of losing the amount. That story did more for the payment plans and forms conversation than any feature description would have.

## What the rep did not do

* Did not lead with features. The statement comparison came first, and every feature shown afterwards answered something the practice had just said.

* Did not oversell the roadmap. When asked about gaps, the answer was honest about what exists today and what is planned, with no delivery dates promised.

* Did not treat a limitation as a dealbreaker. When a workflow did not fit the practice exactly, the response was to name what Adit Pay does well instead and let the training call handle the rest.

# Play 1\. Recover the balances already sitting in their AR

This is the fastest money in the product and the one almost nobody leads with. Run it first if the practice has outstanding patient balances, which is nearly all of them.

## What it is

Bulk Payment Requests, in the Practice Analytics module. Build a filtered list of patients with outstanding balances, then send every one of them a personalised payment link by text, email or both, in a single action. Payment Reminders then chase automatically by SMS and email until the balance is paid, and stop the moment it is.

The alternative a practice is living with today is someone sending text-to-pay links one at a time, which takes days or weeks and quietly never finishes.

## What it is worth

Take their outstanding balance figure and work from that rather than an invented recovery rate. If a practice recovers \$20,000 they were not going to chase, collecting it by bank payment costs them \$275 against \$610 on card, and the collection itself took one action rather than three weeks of front desk time.

The reminders are the part that compounds. A payment link lives for 120 days by default, and every reminder inside that window is revenue that would otherwise have aged out.

## The words

*How much do you currently have sitting in unpaid patient balances? And who is chasing it? In Adit you build the list once, send every patient a payment link in one action, and the system chases them by text and email until they pay. It stops the moment they do.*

## Who to run it on

Everyone, but it is strongest on a practice that has just mentioned ageing AR, a front desk that is short staffed, or a recent clean-up effort somebody abandoned. For Upgrades this is the single best first ask, because it uses a module they already pay for and produces money inside a week.

## The objection

"Our patients will find that pushy." The reminders stop on payment, they go to people who already owe the practice money for treatment already delivered, and the practice controls the cadence and the wording. Compare it to the alternative, which is a statement in the post that nobody opens.

# Play 2\. The terminal

This gets skipped because a terminal reads as hardware. It is not. It is a rate decision with a piece of hardware attached, and on most practices it is the single easiest money in the playbook.

## The thing to understand first

Card present is 2.25% plus \$0.20. Card not present is 2.90% plus \$0.30. A practice that takes a physical card at the front desk and types the number into a screen is paying card-not-present rates on a card-present transaction. That is 0.65% plus \$0.10 more on every one of those transactions, for no reason other than that nobody set a terminal up.

Be precise about what counts, because getting this wrong in front of a client is embarrassing. Card present means the card was tapped, swiped or inserted on the terminal. Everything else is card not present: text-to-pay, manual entry, the website, payment plans, subscriptions, and recurring charges against a card on file. A practice that says most of its payments are automatic charges to a saved card is telling you they are almost entirely card not present, and the terminal play only applies to whatever walk-in volume sits alongside that.

## What it is worth

A practice running \$30,000 a month in the office across roughly 300 transactions pays \$960 keyed and \$735 on a terminal. That is \$225 a month, or \$2,700 a year, from a one-off purchase.

Ask for their in-office card volume and transaction count and do it in front of them. If they do not know the split, open Insights and look at in-office against online, which answers it in seconds.

## The hardware, which sells itself if you say it properly

* **It is \$150, once. That includes shipping and tax.** There is nothing else to pay, ever.

* **The practice owns it.** No rental, no lease, no recurring charge, no leasing company anywhere in the arrangement.

* **It is the S700 or S710. The current models, and better devices than the older terminal that used to sell for \$250.**

* **One year warranty.** If it fails and we cannot troubleshoot it, we replace it. Physical damage is the only exclusion.

* **Thirty day returns** from the date the order is placed.

* **It is wireless.** Use it anywhere in the practice. It also works offsite, at a health fair or a school screening, as long as the terminal and the laptop are on the same network.

## The fact that makes the \$150 land

Adit pays around \$350 per unit to the manufacturer and sells it for \$150. We take a loss on every terminal because we would rather the practice be on the lower card-present rate than protect a hardware margin.

Say that plainly. A practice that has been quoted \$600 for a terminal, or pushed into a 48 month lease costing \$1,773.60 on a \$439 device, understands immediately what kind of arrangement they have been in.

## The second argument, which is better than the first

If the practice runs Convenience Fees, the terminal also makes the fee smaller for the patient, because the fee tracks the processing rate. On a \$500 payment the patient pays \$511.71 in the office against \$515.24 if the card is keyed. The patient saves \$3.53 and the practice still nets the full \$500 either way.

That turns the terminal from a cost conversation into a patient experience one, which is far easier to have with an owner who worries about how fees look.

## The words

*When a patient hands you a card at the desk, does it go through a terminal or does someone type the number in? If it is typed, you are paying online rates on an in-person payment. That is 0.65% plus ten cents more every time. The terminal is \$150 once, including shipping, with a year warranty, and you own it. We pay about \$350 for it, so we are losing money on the hardware. We would rather you were on the lower rate.*

## Closing it on the call

This is the easiest thing in the playbook to close in the moment, because \$150 does not need a committee. Confirm the shipping address and whose name goes on the package, then place the order while you are still on the phone. The charge appears on the card on file within a day.

Worth setting expectations on delivery, particularly around holidays, when courier priority requests are usually refused. And say clearly that everything except in-office payments works immediately. Text-to-pay, payment plans, subscriptions and manual entry are all live the moment setup finishes, so nobody is waiting on the box to start collecting.

## The objections

### *We do not take many cards in the office*

Ask for the number rather than accepting the impression. Practices routinely underestimate this, and Insights answers it in seconds.

### *We already have a terminal*

They can keep it. Running two merchant accounts is entirely normal and nothing stops a practice using both. Frame the Adit terminal as the one they reach for on the payments that matter, rather than as a replacement decision they have to make today.

# Play 3\. Move the big treatment plans to bank payments

Bank payments are not a general cost saving. They are a large balance saving, and pitching them as anything else weakens them.

## The crossover

ACH is 0.5% plus \$1.75. Card not present is 2.90% plus \$0.30. The flat \$1.75 dominates on small tickets, so the two cross at about \$60. Below that a card is cheaper. Above it, bank wins and the gap widens with the size of the balance.

On \$200 the practice keeps \$3.35 more. On \$1,000 it keeps \$22.55 more. On a \$5,000 treatment plan it keeps \$118.55 more.

## What it is worth

A practice closing 8 large cases a month at \$5,000 keeps roughly \$948 a month, or \$11,380 a year, purely by taking those payments by bank instead of card. Ask how many big cases they close and multiply.

## Why this is the only lever on payment plans

Convenience Fees never apply to payment plans or subscriptions. Plan balances are the large ones, so on exactly the payments where card fees hurt most, the fee cannot be passed to the patient. Bank payments are the only way to reduce that cost, which makes this play and Play 5 natural partners.

## The words

*On a \$5,000 case, taking it on a card costs you \$145. Taking the same payment from the patient’s bank account costs you \$26.75. That is \$118 back on one case, and you cannot pass the fee through on a payment plan, so this is the only lever you have on your biggest balances.*

## The mechanics to get right

* **The practice has to send a payment method request first.** The patient links their bank through it. Only then can a payment be charged. Say this early or it becomes a support ticket.

* **Adding and verifying a bank account is free.** No fee, no hold.

* **It settles in 1 to 4 business days.** Slower than a card. Raise it before they discover it.

* **The 0.5% plus \$1.75 cannot be passed to the patient.** The practice carries it. Do not let anyone imply otherwise.

## The objection

"Our patients will not link a bank account." Some will not. It only has to work on the large balances to pay for itself, and one \$5,000 case a month covers it. Also worth saying: the patient logs into their bank to verify rather than typing account numbers off a cheque, which is both faster and safer than the alternative they are imagining.

# Play 4\. Stop them funding the card fees

The Convenience Fee play. Strong, and the one with the most ways to get it wrong, so the mechanics section below is not optional reading.

## The framing that works

Do not open with the word fee. Open with what the practice is losing today. When a patient pays \$100 by card, the practice does not receive \$100. It receives \$96.80, because the processing cost comes out of it. With Convenience Fees on, that cost goes to the patient instead and the practice receives the full amount it billed, exactly as it would with cash or a cheque.

## What it is worth

A practice running \$30,000 a month in card-not-present volume is paying about \$960 a month in processing. If 85% of that volume is eligible one-time card payments, which is the default assumption Statement IQ uses, switching Convenience Fees on recovers around \$816 a month, or roughly \$9,792 a year.

That is not a saving. It is money the practice was already earning and handing to a processor.

## The words

*Right now when a patient pays you \$100 on a card, you get \$96.80. The rest goes to processing. Convenience Fees pass that cost to the patient instead, so you receive the full \$100, the same as cash. On your volume that is around \$9,800 a year you are currently absorbing.*

## The fee is grossed up, and you must explain that before they see it

The fee is calculated so the practice nets the billed amount after processing is deducted, which makes it slightly larger than a flat percentage on top. A \$1,000 service carries a \$30.18 fee, so the patient pays \$1,030.18 and the practice receives \$1,000. On \$100 it is \$102.51 in the office or \$103.30 keyed.

Almost every query about a fee amount comes from a practice expecting a simple percentage and finding a grossed-up figure. Say it first and it never becomes a complaint.

## The objections

### *My patients will be upset*

The patient is not being charged more for the visit. The practice has stopped covering the cost of the payment method the patient chose. And the patient always has a fee-free route, in person or by bank where that is offered.

### *Is this not just a surcharge?*

No, and the distinction is legal rather than cosmetic. A surcharge can only be applied to credit cards and is not legal on debit at all. A Convenience Fee applies uniformly to credit and debit alike and is disclosed to the patient as its own line before they pay. That uniformity is exactly what puts it in a separate, compliant category.

### *We prefer cash and cheque, card fees eat the small tickets*

That is the problem this removes. With the fee on, a card payment nets the same as cash, so there is no longer a reason to steer patients away from cards on small amounts.



*Whether the fee applies to any given payment. Check this before you promise anything*

# Play 5\. Win the cases they are losing on affordability

The only play here that grows revenue rather than reducing cost, which makes it the one that lands with a practice owner who is not interested in shaving basis points.

## What it is

Payment Plans split a balance across scheduled installments. Subscriptions handle recurring memberships. CareCredit appears as a financing option on text-to-pay links once the practice is a connected CareCredit merchant, so financing lives in the same flow rather than in a separate system.

## The argument

A practice presenting a \$5,000 treatment plan and hearing "I need to think about it" is usually hearing an affordability objection rather than a clinical one. A plan turns that into a monthly number the patient can say yes to. One additional case accepted per month at \$5,000 is \$60,000 a year, which dwarfs every cost saving elsewhere in this playbook.

Pair it with Play 3\. Plans carry no Convenience Fee, so taking the installments by bank is the only way to protect the margin on them.

## The words

*When a patient says they need to think about a treatment plan, how often is that really about money? If you could turn a \$5,000 case into a monthly payment on the spot, without sending them to a finance application, how many more of those would you close in a year?*

## The objection

"We already use CareCredit." Good, and they keep it. It shows up inside the payment link rather than as a separate system, and it never carries a Convenience Fee. The pitch is not replacement, it is removing the second login and giving them an in-house plan option for patients CareCredit declines.

# Play 6\. Show them their own numbers

A retention and expansion play rather than a closing one. Use it when a practice is already on Adit Pay and you want a reason to be in the conversation, or when you need evidence for one of the other plays.

## What Insights gives them

Adit Pay, then Insights. Amount processed, convenience fees collected, processing fees, net processed, total unpaid balance and refunds, each compared against the previous period. Beneath that, a breakdown of how patients actually pay: in office against online, card against bank, cash and cheque, and one-time payments against plans and subscriptions. Then disputes by status, and trend charts by amount or count.

## Why it matters to you rather than to them

It is the evidence source for every other play. The in-office against online split tells you whether Play 2 is worth running. The card against bank split tells you how much of Play 3 is still on the table. Total unpaid balance is the opening number for Play 1\. Pull the dashboard up on the call and the practice watches you find the money rather than being told about it.

## The words

*Can we open Insights for a second? I want to look at two numbers with you: how much of your card volume is in the office, and what your unpaid balance total is. Those two usually tell us where the money is.*

# Play 7\. Displace the incumbent processor

The Sales play. Slower than the others because it depends on documents, but it is the one that moves a practice off a competitor.

## Do not do the maths by hand

Statement IQ reads a practice’s current processing statement, extracts volume, transaction count, total fees and processor using AI, and calculates what they would pay on Adit Pay instead. A raw PDF becomes a shareable savings analysis in under a minute. Sign in with your adit.com Google account.

* New Analysis, then upload. PDF, scanned images, screenshots, XLSX and CSV, up to 5 MB.

* Analyze with AI, then check the extracted values. The usual correction is a missed total on a multi-page statement.

* Set the terminal against online split to match how they really take payments, because the two rates differ.

* Confirm and calculate. It autosaves to the shared dashboard and pushes to Zoho CRM against an AP ID.

## Reading the answer

Monthly Savings is their current fees minus ours. A 10% or better reduction is a strong case you lead with on the dollar figure. Between 5% and 10%, pair it with a platform argument. Under 5%, fee savings will not close it on their own.

A negative number is a real answer, not a bug. It means they are already on a cheap high-volume interchange-plus plan. Stop selling on price and move to Plays 1, 2 and 5, which do not depend on beating their rate.

The tool also lists named add-on fees it finds, such as PCI compliance or monthly service charges, that Adit Pay does not charge. These are frequently more persuasive than the rate itself, because the practice often did not know they were paying them.

## The effective rate conversation

Total fees divided by total volume. A practice processing \$42,000 with \$1,310 in total charges is on 3.12%, and will tell you they are on 1.63% because that is the qualified rate on the front page. Audited statements on one dental platform showed a 1.63% headline producing 2.78% and 3.22% effective. On \$42,000 a month that gap is \$626 a month, or \$7,512 a year.

## The ask that gets you the document

*Send me one processing statement and, if you lease a terminal, the lease agreement. I will come back with your real effective rate, what is left on the lease, and what bank payments would have saved you on last quarter’s large balances. If it is not compelling, I will tell you.*

## The two contract facts worth having

* **Terminal leases.** \$36.95 a month over 48 non cancellable months is \$1,773.60 on a \$439 device. Ask who owns their terminal and how many months are left.

* **Per location exit fees.** One processor charges \$395 per location. A 6 site group pays \$2,370 to leave. Ask how many locations before anything else.

# Running the plays: Sales against Upgrades

## If you are in Sales

Lead with Play 7, because the practice is on somebody else and the statement is what gets you a second meeting. But do not stop there. Plays 1, 2 and 5 all work regardless of what Statement IQ says, and they are what you fall back to when the savings number comes back thin or negative.

Adit Pay is rarely why a practice took the first call. Bring it in once they have accepted that consolidating tools is worth doing, because it is where an abstract claim becomes something they can picture happening at their front desk on Monday.

## If you are in Upgrades

Start with Play 1\. The practice already pays for Practice Analytics, the money is already theirs, and it produces a result inside a week. Nothing builds permission for the rest of the conversation faster than recovering balances they had written off.

Then Play 2, then Play 6 to find where the rest is. Play 7 is rarely yours, since they are already on Adit Pay.

### *The call you will get most often*

Because Bank/ACH is on by default, your most common opening is not an objection about value. It is a practice noticing a bank payment option they never enabled and asking why it is there. Lead with reassurance rather than a defence. It was enabled automatically as part of the rollout, it was not specific to their account, nothing about their pricing or workflow changed, and it can be turned off in Preferences in about a minute.

Handled well, that call becomes the opening for Plays 1 and 3, because you are already talking about payments and they raised it.

### *Trigger moments worth diarising*

* **A terminal lease ending or a terminal failing.** The best moment there is. Ask when the term ends and put it in the calendar.

* **A processor rate increase notice.** They will mention it as a complaint. Treat it as a buying signal.

* **A contract renewal approaching.** Renewal windows are often narrow and badly specified, so they need to move early.

* **Adding a location.** Eligibility and settings are per location, so this is the natural moment to consolidate.

* **Front desk turnover.** Someone new is learning the manual double entry from scratch, which is when its cost is visible.

* **A held or delayed deposit elsewhere.** Cash flow pain creates urgency that a feature comparison never will.

### *Answers you will need*

* **"Can we get paid out the same day?"** Daily payout is available to every client regardless of size. There is no minimum.

* **"We are happy with our processor."** Happy usually means nothing has gone visibly wrong lately. Ask their effective rate, who actually holds their money, and what response time they are guaranteed. Most cannot answer any of the three.

* **"Not mid year."** Find the renewal or lease end date, diarise it, come back a month before. A booked follow up at the right moment beats pushing at the wrong one.

# Setup, and what happens after the yes

Setup takes about ten minutes and can be done on the call while you are still talking. Do it then rather than scheduling it, because a booked setup call is a deal that can still evaporate.

## Before you start

* **It must be an owner-level user.** Nobody else can complete it, no matter how senior. If you have been talking to an office manager, you need the owner back for this part.

* **Have these ready.** EIN, the exact legal business name registered to that EIN, practice phone and physical address, the owner with the largest stake and their date of birth and social security number, and the practice bank account and routing numbers.

The social security number is the step that causes hesitation, so explain it before they ask. It verifies citizenship, it is removed as soon as the page is submitted, and a photo ID is only required for someone using an ITIN rather than an SSN. Offer to let them stop screen sharing while they type it. That small courtesy defuses the whole moment.

## The steps

* Adit Pay, then Get Started. Set business type to Company and pick the structure, for example multi-member LLC.

* EIN, legal name exactly as registered, practice phone, physical address and postal code.

* The DBA name, which is what patients see on their statement. It has a 22 character limit, so agree the wording rather than guessing.

* Website, including https\:// at the front or it will reject it.

* Owner details: name, job title, email, mobile, date of birth, SSN and home address. The email matters because the 1099-K goes there every year.

* Bank account holder name, account number and routing number. Confirm it is the practice account rather than a personal one.

* Payout schedule, payout notification emails, and transaction notification emails. Multiple addresses are separated by commas with no spaces.

* Switch payment posting from No to Yes if they want payments landing on the ledger.

* Skip terminal registration if the terminal has not arrived, then Start Using Adit Pay.

## Set expectations on the last screen

Two things to say before you hang up. Everything except in-office terminal payments works immediately, so text-to-pay, payment plans, subscriptions and manual entry are all live now. And they can carry on using their existing in-office method until the terminal arrives, or indefinitely if they want. Nothing forces them to stop.

They will also get an email for every payout and every transaction, refund and dispute. Do not let them disable transaction notifications, but do suggest pointing them at a shared practice inbox rather than the owner personally, or they will turn them off within a week.

## Book the training before you leave the call

Setup is not adoption. A ninety minute training session with the Adit Pay team is what turns a configured account into one that actually collects. Get a name and a time for it while you still have their attention, and hand over a direct number so the coordinator can reach someone without going through a queue.

If they cannot commit to a slot, agree a specific follow-up date out loud and say when you will call. A vague "I will chase you" is how a live account goes quiet.

# Things nobody should say

Each of these is a way to lose a conversation that was otherwise going well, because each is a claim that will not survive the practice checking it.

## Do not say the Convenience Fee posts to the ledger

It does not. Only the underlying service amount posts. The fee is tracked in Adit Pay reporting instead. Promising otherwise creates a reconciliation problem the practice will discover in their first month.

## Do not call it a surcharge, or let a client call it one unchallenged

Correct it, using the credit and debit distinction in the earlier section. This is compliance-adjacent and the practice may repeat what you told them to their own advisers.

## Do not promise Convenience Fees where they are not available

Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts and Canada are excluded, and eligibility is assessed per location from the address on the connected payment account. A multi-location group may have some locations eligible and others not. Check before promising.

## Do not promise the fee on plans, subscriptions, ACH, CareCredit or HSA and FSA cards

None of them carry it, ever. One-time card payments only.

## Do not tell a practice their processor is not HIPAA compliant

Under federal guidance a financial institution that simply processes payment transactions is generally not a business associate, so declining to sign a business associate agreement is not evidence of non compliance. The real question is whether the payment product touches protected health information beyond the transaction itself. Ask that. Do not accuse.

## Do not attack the two competitors who are honest on price

One publishes a flat 2.79%. Another publishes its entire rate card with no contract and no lease. Pretending we beat a published number we have not verified loses the deal permanently. Move to workflow, ledger posting and structural risk instead.

## Do not attack the two whose compliance posture is strong

One carries HITRUST and SOC 2 Type 2 and markets compliance on every tier. The other does sign a business associate agreement for its relevant services. Compete with them on integration depth and single vendor accountability, which are arguments we win.

## Do not quote a competitor rate as fact

Every competitor figure here is either vendor published or a third party audit of somebody else’s statement. Neither is the rate in front of your prospect. Ask for their statement instead of telling them what they pay.

## Do not raise our own internal matters

Internal escalations, incidents and support cases have no place in a prospect or customer conversation.

## What to escalate rather than answer

* Any specific account showing behaviour you did not expect.

* Questions about editing against recreating a payment request when a fee needs removing.

* Anything compliance-adjacent you are not fully confident about. Checking is cheaper than guessing.
