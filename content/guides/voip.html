Part 1: Support guide

This guide gives T1 CS what it needs to take basic Adit Voice calls and chats: where each setting lives, the requests we can finish on the first call, the checks the Phones team runs in order, and when a ticket goes to the VoIP queue. It is built from the two T1 VoIP training sessions, the Adit Voice master document, the VoIP assessment, and the Phones team's Zoho Desk tickets and recorded client calls.

Part 2 lists the queries clients raised with the Phones team and how the Phones agents resolved each one, with the Zoho Desk ticket numbers to read.

Your scope and approach

T1 handles configuration requests and first-line checks; the Phones team (VoIP queue) handles anything that needs the phone server, device management or the developers. As Shubham put it in training, most of what clients ask can be closed in two to five minutes once you know where the setting lives.

What T1 resolves: greeting and voicemail changes, business hours and schedules, call override on or off, line labels and user association, blocked callers, hold music, audio library uploads, call recording toggles, extra voicemail boxes on V4 accounts, eFax how-to, finding calls and recordings in Call Tracking, softphone and mobile app setup, DND, and basic phone checks such as power, cable, Wi-Fi, reboot and cordless re-pairing.

What goes to the VoIP queue: phones that stay "Account not registered" after basic checks, several phones down at once, any factory reset through GDMS, voicemail boxes on V2 or V3, missing recordings when both recording toggles are on, headset faults, repeating audio problems or call drops, and fax failures that repeat across different numbers. The full list is in the hand-over section.

For a how-to or change request

  1. Understand what the client wants, including which hours or which branch it applies to.

  2. Find the right feature.

  3. Take a screenshot of the current setup and add it to the ticket comments before you change anything.

  4. Make the change and click Update. Unsaved call flow changes are silently lost.

  5. Place a test call and check the result.

  6. Tell the client anything they need to do themselves, such as rebooting a phone, and confirm it is resolved.

For a fault

The Phones team follows the same order every time: Identify, check basics, reproduce, isolate, fix, verify, document.

  1. Identify: the device model, the user, the extension, the exact symptom or on-screen error, and when it started. Confirm the phone is a supported model before troubleshooting it.

  2. Check basics: power, network, registration, permissions, settings and physical connections.

  3. Reproduce: place a controlled test call to see the problem yourself.

  4. Isolate: decide whether it is the device, the user, the network, the configuration or the service.

  5. Fix: apply only an approved fix. A factory reset is never the first step.

  6. Verify: re-test the original function, in both directions for audio.

  7. Document: symptoms, checks done, action taken and final status in the ticket.

Things that confuse new agents:

  • Extensions starting with 0 (001, 007) are softphones in the Adit app. Extensions starting with 1 (101 to 109) are physical phones.

  • If a user is associated with a hard phone and turns on the softphone, the softphone can take the same extension number instead of a new 00x number.

  • In Lines, every cordless handset shows the base model DP750, never DP720. Five DP750 rows means one base with five handsets.

  • The Voice version (V2, V3 or V4) shows on the account. It matters for extra voicemail boxes and for the lunch-hours schedule issue.

  • Every account comes with one landline number and one fax number. Tracking numbers are extra numbers that can have their own call flow.

User roles

Owner and Manager users can change settings, including schedules and call override. Staff users cannot, so a staff member who asks to change hours needs an owner to raise their role first (385817, 395616). Only Owner and Manager users can port numbers or buy tracking and fax numbers. Read-only users can view but not edit.

Where settings live

Everything below is under the Settings gear (top right) in the Voice section. On multi-location accounts you must pick the location first.

  • Phone System: the landline and fax numbers for each location. Click the landline number to open its call flow, which also holds the Record Inbound Calls toggle. Click the fax number to set where incoming faxes are emailed.

  • Call Flow Templates: saved extra call flows, and Copy Flow to apply one to a number.

  • Lines / Prefixes: each extension's label, user, voicemail box, model and MAC address.

  • Blocked Callers, Hold Music, Call Override, Audio Library, Call Recording (outbound) and Voicemail Boxes each have their own page.

  • Tracking Numbers: route each tracking number to a call flow.

Call Tracking is the headset-with-lines icon on the left panel of the home screen. Orange icons are outbound calls, green are inbound, red are missed calls and voicemails. A solid blue recording icon means nobody has played that recording yet.

How a call flow works

Every inbound call checks call override first, then runs the call flow from the top: greeting, schedule, and the branch that matches the day and time. Most "phones not ringing" calls come down to one step in this path being set the wrong way.

The order a call follows:

  1. Incoming call.

  2. Is call override on? If yes, the call goes straight to the override destination (a message, a recording or a forward) and the call flow is skipped.

  3. If no, the greeting plays. It is always first in the flow.

  4. The schedule checks the day and time and sends the call to the matching branch.

  5. Branch A (before lunch, open hours): Simulring to lines, users or a cell; if no one answers, voicemail, which is always last.

  6. Branch B (lunch, closed): the lunch voicemail message.

  7. Branch C (after lunch, open hours): Simulring; if no one answers, voicemail.

  8. Any Other Time (after hours and weekends): voicemail, a menu, or an AI answering line.

This is the lunch-closure layout the Phones team now uses: three branches for before lunch, lunch and after lunch, with each weekday entered once per branch. Rules from the training:

  • Greeting always goes first and voicemail always last. A voicemail placed at the top plays to every caller and then hangs up.

  • Ring rings one line or one external number. Simulring rings several lines, users or external numbers at once, and choosing a user rings every extension associated with that user.

  • Ring time is about 5 seconds per ring, with a minimum of 10 seconds and a maximum of 60.

  • Anything a schedule branch does not cover falls into Any Other Time.

  • Do not insert a new step between existing steps; it can make the flow unstable. Take a screenshot, clear the flow and rebuild it in the right order. Changing times inside a schedule step is fine.

  • Nothing is saved until you click Update.

Requests you can finish on the first call

These are the requests the Phones team listed as first call resolution work. Each one ends the same way: click Update or Save, then test.

Change a phone's label or who it belongs to

Labels are the names shown on the phone, such as "Front Desk 1", which make transfers easier.

  1. Voice, then Lines / Prefixes.

  2. Click the pencil next to the extension. Most fields are locked because asset management controls them.

  3. Change the label, or pick a user under user association, and save.

  4. Ask the client to reboot that phone: Menu, scroll to the bottom, Reboot. If they cannot find it, unplug and replug. The new label does not appear, and the phone can misbehave, until it reboots.

Only associate a user with an extension if that person always sits at that phone. Most practices rotate desks, so lines usually stay under the practice name.

Change the greeting or voicemail message

  1. Upload or record the audio first if it is new (see Audio Library below).

  2. Voice, then Phone System, pick the location and click the landline number.

  3. For the greeting, open the Greeting step at the top. For voicemail, scroll to the Voicemail step at the end of the branch.

  4. Choose Play Recording and pick the file, or Read Message and type text for the system to read in a computer voice.

  5. Click Save inside the step, then Update at the bottom of the call flow.

Always ask which voicemail they mean. Business hours, lunch and "Any Other Time" (after hours) each have their own voicemail, and changing the wrong one is a common repeat call (409833, 405363).

Upload or record audio

Voice, then Audio Library. Use Record Your Own with a headset or microphone connected, name the file, record and click Upload. Or use Upload to add a file from the computer. The mobile app can also record under Settings, then Audio Library. The file only appears in call flow drop-downs once it is in the library.

Change business hours, lunch or a one-off day

  1. Open the call flow and click the Schedule step.

  2. Branch A holds the open hours. Anything not listed falls into "Any Other Time", which is after hours.

  3. Update the days and times and click Update.

For offices that close for lunch, use three branches: A before lunch, B lunch (voicemail only), C after lunch, with each weekday entered once per branch. The older setup that listed the same day twice in branch A causes phones not to ring, especially on V4.

For a one-off change, such as staff arriving at 6 AM when the schedule opens at 7, change the time, click Update, and remind them to change it back, or show them how on a remote session. Friday afternoon "calls go to voicemail" tickets are often a practice working later than its Friday closing time.

Turn call override on or off

Call override sends every call somewhere else for a while, for holidays, weather closures or unexpected closures.

  1. Voice, then Call Override, and pick the location (it can also be toggled from the Call Pop settings).

  2. Toggle it on and accept the popup.

  3. Choose Read Message, Play Recording or Call Forward to an external number, then click Update.

Remind the client it stays on until someone turns it off. A forgotten override is the first thing to check when a practice says the phones stopped ringing after a holiday (410686, 405215). Override cannot offer menu options. If they want "press 1 for the doctor, press 2 for voicemail" while closed, build a holiday call flow instead (below). The same external number can sit in both the call flow and the override without a conflict.

Switch between saved call flows

Use Call Flow Templates when a practice has alternate-day schedules, a holiday menu, or wants a backup before changes.

  1. Voice, then Call Flow Templates, pick the location, Create Call Flow, build and save it. Name it clearly, for example "Mon Wed Fri" or "Holiday".

  2. To apply it, open the template, click Copy Flow at the bottom, pick the number and click Apply. The top number is usually the main line.

  3. To go back, apply the original flow the same way. For complex flows, save a copy of the main flow as a template first, or there is nothing to switch back to.

Editing a template does not change the live flow until you Copy Flow. A tracking number can use a different template under Tracking Numbers, pencil, Route To, Call Flow.

Add a menu or an external number

For a menu, add a Greeting that reads the options, then a Menu step with each option pointing to an extension or voicemail, then Update. For an external number, such as a cell phone or AI answering service, add a Ring step with the external number and a ring time, then Update (403525).

Block a caller and change hold music

Voice, then Blocked Callers, pick the location, enter the number and click Block. Voice, then Hold Music, pick the location, Custom Hold Music, select audio from the library and click Update.

Turn call recording on or off

Inbound and outbound recording live in different places. Inbound is per number: open the landline's call flow and use the Record Inbound Calls toggle. Outbound is per location: Voice, then Call Recording. Ask which direction the client means before changing anything; most practices keep both on.

Add a voicemail box

  1. Check the Voice version on the account. On V4 you can switch on multiple voicemail boxes yourself. On V2 or V3 send it to the VoIP queue, because the developers must enable it.

  2. Voice, then Voicemail Boxes, Add Voicemail Box. Name it, choose which users can see it, and add email or text alerts.

  3. Assign the box in the call flow at the point where voicemail should play.

  4. Assign the same box to the extension in Lines / Prefixes. It must match in both places, or messages land in the default box (390072, 411976, 385942).

Send and track an eFax

  1. Home screen, blue + icon, fax machine icon.

  2. Choose the contact type, enter name and fax number, and attach the document as a PDF.

  3. Check the number and the attachment, then Send. Do not leave the fax window open for 20 minutes or more, or it times out and shows as failed.

To see sent and received faxes, open Call Tracking, then Fax. Blue icons are incoming, orange are outgoing. A failed fax shows an i icon; hover over it to see why. To email incoming faxes, open the fax number under Phone System and add an address in "Send incoming faxes to". Staff who cannot open eFax need Call Tracking access on their user profile (399338).

Find a call or play a recording

Call Tracking, Calls. Set the date range first, then use filters (direction, missed, voicemail, extension) or search by the caller's number. Open the call to see the extension that answered, the duration and the recording. Voicemails have a transcript and a short AI summary on the call detail page. Archived voicemails move to the Archived tab and can be unarchived.

Using the phones and apps

Clients often call simply to ask how to do something on the phone. Grandstream and Yealink work almost the same way; the button positions differ by model.

Desk and cordless phones

  • Hold: press the Hold (two vertical bars) key; press it again to resume. On a DP720 handset, use the right soft key.

  • Transfer: press Transfer, dial the extension, then press Transfer again. Clients who press it only once leave the caller on hold until the call drops. Blind transfer sends the call straight through; attended transfer lets them speak to the colleague first, then press Transfer to hand over.

  • Park: press the Park key; a red light shows on every phone and the call can be picked up from any phone with the same key. Grandstream park slots are 901 and 902. On a DP720, park by blind transferring to 901 or 902. Yealink phones use the green park keys or *7.

  • Internal calls: dial the extension directly (for example 103), or use Intercom or 999 on Grandstream. Hard phones and softphones can call each other.

  • Conference: press Conference, dial the second person, then Conference again once they answer.

  • Voicemail on the phone: press the Message key and follow the prompts. The default password is usually the extension number.

  • Mute: a short press. If a patient says they cannot hear the staff member, check mute first.

  • DND: press and hold Mute for 5 to 7 seconds. A red circle with a minus sign means the phone will not ring. Hold Mute again to turn it off. The same works on cordless handsets.

  • Reboot: Menu (the round centre button), scroll to the bottom, Reboot. Unplug and replug if they cannot find it.

  • Ringtone: centre button, Preferences, Sounds, choose a ringtone with left and right, then Save.

Cables and Wi-Fi

The wall cable goes into the LAN port (three small boxes on Grandstream, labelled Internet on Yealink). The port with a computer icon passes internet on to the PC, so if the phone goes offline the PC can lose internet too. Power is a round-pin adapter, or PoE where the switch supports it.

The GRP2616 and Yealink T53W and T54W can join Wi-Fi when there is no free wall port. GRP2616: Menu, System, Wi-Fi settings, enable, Scan, choose the network and enter the password; a Wi-Fi icon appears top right when connected. Yealink: Menu, Network, WiFi. The GRP2613 cannot use Wi-Fi.

Headset

Adit supplies the Plantronics CS540. To connect it, unplug the handset's spiral cable from the back of the phone, connect the supplied cable from that port to the headset base, plug the handset cable into the small box on that cable, and connect the base's power. Headset faults go to the VoIP queue.

Softphone on the computer

The desktop app downloads from the profile menu (top right) on adit.com, Download Desktop App. The softphone is the Call Pop icon at the top.

  • Turn it on: toggle the softphone in Call Pop, pick the line and save. It must be green.

  • It must also be in the call flow, by line or by user name, or it will never ring and the call pop will not show (391313, 408456, 413248).

  • Pause calls without logging out: click the half-moon for DND. Turn it off completely from the line drop-down, Turn Off Softphone.

  • A red phone icon with a slash means the computer has no microphone. Plug in a headset or earbuds (402835).

  • Call Pop settings also hold call waiting and park notifications.

Adit mobile app

  • Download "Adit" from the App Store or Play Store. Staff outside the countries Adit allows may not see the app in their store.

  • Allow microphone and notifications. On iPhone allow Background App Refresh; on Android turn off battery optimisation for Adit. Missing permissions are the main reason calls do not ring on mobile.

  • To make a call, use the dial pad at the top right or the Patients tab.

  • A user logged in on both desktop and mobile with the same extension rings on both. If a doctor does not want office calls on their cell, turn on DND in the mobile app (Settings, Do Not Disturb). The mobile app has no softphone toggle, only DND (409745, 394450).

  • If the user logs in with someone else's credentials, the call flow must list that account's name or extension, not theirs.

Phones not ringing or calls going straight to voicemail

T1 will hear this complaint often, and in many tickets the cause was a setting T1 can fix. Before you start, ask whether it is one phone or all of them, and whether the phone can still make outbound calls.

Work through these checks in order. The first one that answers yes is usually the cause.

  1. Is call override on? Check Voice, Call Override, or the Call Pop settings. If yes, turn it off and click Update. This is often the cause after a holiday.

  2. Is a phone offline or unregistered? The screen shows No Account, an X icon or offline. If it is one phone, check the cable, Wi-Fi and reboot. If it is every phone, treat it as an outage and send it to the VoIP queue.

  3. Is the phone on DND? A red circle with a minus sign shows on screen. Hold Mute for 5 to 7 seconds. On the softphone click the half-moon; on mobile use Settings.

  4. Is the call outside the schedule? Check the practice's time zone and the day. Correct the times and click Update. A practice working past its Friday closing time is a frequent cause.

  5. Is the extension missing from the branch? The line, user or softphone must be in the Ring or Simulring step. Add it and click Update. This also fixes a missing call pop.

  6. Is the account on V4 with lunch inside one branch, meaning the same weekday is listed twice in branch A? Split it into branches A, B and C: before lunch, lunch, after lunch.

  7. Did the caller hang up within seconds? Call Tracking shows a very short missed call. Explain this to the client; no change is needed (408155, 395952).

  8. Still not ringing: send it to the VoIP queue with call examples and what you checked.

A softphone being on or off never affects the desk phones; they are separate endpoints. When several checks look fine but calls still go to voicemail, look again at the times in the schedule against the practice's local time before escalating.

Other repeat issues and how they were fixed

Ticket numbers in brackets are Zoho Desk tickets where you can read the Phones team's full notes.

One desk phone offline, frozen, or showing "No Account"

  1. Ask what the screen says and whether one phone or all are affected. If every phone shows "Account not registered" at once, it is an outage or server issue: send it to the VoIP queue straight away.

  2. Check the cable at both ends, swap the cable, and make sure it is in the LAN port, not the PC port (413854, 392554). An IP of 0.0.0.0 means the phone is getting no network from the office, so the problem is their cabling or internet (395856).

  3. On a GRP2616 or Yealink, try Wi-Fi if there is no working wall port; reconnect Wi-Fi if a reboot dropped it (401247, 399441).

  4. Reboot, or power cycle and leave it unplugged for a few minutes (391663).

  5. If it is still offline or unregistered, send it to the VoIP queue. They check the phone in GDMS (Grandstream) or the Yealink portal, push a reboot, reprovision or update firmware (403507, 409314, 406025, 385833).

Do not factory reset through GDMS without the Phones team agreeing first: a reset can wipe the phone's provisioning so it comes online but does not work. A manual reset (Grandstream: centre button, Settings, System Settings, Factory Reset; Yealink: hold OK for 5 seconds) is only for a frozen phone when the Phones team has asked for it, and "Account not registered" afterwards always goes to them. A phone that is online and registered but still not working also goes to them.

Cordless phone not working or "Out of range"

The base is the antenna; if it is offline every handset on it stops. The DP750 base has five lights: power, internet, registration, and two for handsets. A second light that is not solid means the base has no internet.

  1. Check the base has power and its network cable is in; restart the base (395853, 403539).

  2. Check the handset for DND (hold Mute 5 to 7 seconds) and for a charged battery; a battery pull and restart often fixes it (385443, 396130, 409839).

  3. Re-pair the handset: hold the base's single back button for 5 to 7 seconds to open pairing, then on a DP720 press the left soft key, Registration, Register, choose Base 1, Subscribe. On Yealink, hold the base button with the Wi-Fi symbol, then register from the handset. The Phones team can also open pairing remotely from GDMS.

  4. If it still says out of range after pairing, ask how far the handset is from the base. Walls cut the range a lot, so keep handsets reasonably close.

"Call release 502" or the base losing its SIP registration needs the Phones team (395507, 394925, 412322).

Adit app or softphone problems

  • App shows Offline: completely close and reopen it, log in again, then check the network, that the softphone is on, that the right line is picked, and the permissions, then test a call. If the error persists on desktop, uninstall from Control Panel and reinstall; on mobile, delete and reinstall.

  • Call pop not showing: log out and back in, check notification settings, update Chrome, and confirm a user is assigned to the extension (409645, 394443, 397066, 414010).

  • Softphone rings but cannot call, or works on one laptop but not another: the computer is the problem, so check its microphone and audio devices (396396).

  • Mobile app not ringing: permissions, battery optimisation on Android, and that the extension is in the call flow (393513).

Users and lines

A user cannot be deleted while they still have an active phone line. Remove or reassign the line first, then delete the user (393155, 392746, 402631, 385704).

Call park

Missing park keys on some phones are fixed by the Phones team re-pushing the configuration and rebooting (401483, 393879, 411934). Sometimes the client is simply pressing the wrong key on a new phone (404062). If park fails on several phones or locations on the same day, it is likely a platform issue: escalate rather than troubleshoot (386404).

Audio problems and dropped calls

Many audio tickets close with no fault found because it was one call or the caller's own line (391176, 403456). Before escalating, collect a pattern: two or three examples with time, number and extension; whether it is all phones or one; wired or Wi-Fi; and whether it happens at a certain time of day.

Tell apart delayed audio and one-way audio. If the caller says "hello, hello" and hangs up, ask the practice to stay on the line 10 seconds or more next time; true one-way audio is rare. Calls that drop after being on hold or mute are a known pattern the developers handle.

Causes found so far: SIP ALG switched on in the client's router (385555), the client's internet provider (405622, 389744), old firmware (393729, 412787), and server-side issues fixed by the Phones team or developers (402243, 407718). Codec, jitter buffer and firewall changes are done by the Phones team, not T1.

Caller ID shows as spam, wrong caller name, robocalls

Numbers flagged "Potential Spam" are re-registered with the anti-spam databases by the Phones team; tell the client it takes 5 to 7 days to clear (387923, 385042, 396897). A wrong outbound business name is a CNAM update, which can take up to 72 hours (401862, 387454). For a surge of robocalls, add a "press 1 to reach the office" step to the call flow (403495) or block the specific numbers.

eFax failures

Hover over the i icon on the failed fax in Call Tracking to read the reason.

  • Timed out: the fax window was left open too long. Send it again straight away.

  • Declined by the receiver or receiver carrier: the problem is on the recipient's side, for example their fax line was busy (394818).

  • Improper format: the file was not a PDF.

  • File too large: compress the PDF (411106).

  • Fax arrived but no email: check the email in "Send incoming faxes to" (397268).

If the same error repeats across different numbers over several days, send it to the VoIP queue with examples.

Voicemail and recordings

Voicemails not loading in the app are fixed by logging out and in (398615). Voicemails visible in Call Tracking but not on the desk phone mean the extension has no voicemail box assigned (390072). A message light that stays on after messages are heard has needed a developer fix before (387351, 385765). If a recording is missing while both recording toggles are on, send it to the VoIP queue with the call details, as it may be a known bug.

Time, labels and speed dials

Wrong time or time zone on the phones (395756, 411540) and speed dial or line key setup (396070) are done by the Phones team from the device portal. Take the request with the exact extensions and what they want, then hand it over. Label changes you can do yourself in Lines.

When to hand over to the Phones team

Resolve at T1 when the fix is on the client's side or in the Adit app: power cycles, cables and Wi-Fi, base station restarts, DND, call override, call flow and schedule changes, voicemail greetings, app log-outs and notification settings, user and line clean-up, and eFax file size or access.

Send the ticket to the VoIP queue when:

  • A phone or cordless base still shows "No Account" or "Account not registered" after the cable, Wi-Fi and reboot checks, or a phone is online and registered but still not working.

  • Several phones or every phone at a practice go offline at once.

  • A factory reset through GDMS looks necessary. Ask the Phones team before any reset.

  • Calls go to voicemail although override is off, the phones are online and not on DND, and the call flow and hours look right.

  • The account is on V2 or V3 and needs an extra voicemail box.

  • A recording is missing while both recording toggles are on.

  • Audio problems or drops repeat, sent with your examples.

  • Spam flags, caller name (CNAM), time zone, speed dials or park keys need changing.

  • A headset is faulty, or the message light stays on.

  • Fax failures repeat across different numbers over several days.

  • Anything else you have checked without finding the cause.

In the ticket, include the extension numbers, the phone model, what the screen says, when it started, whether one phone or all are affected, what you already checked, any screenshot of the call flow before you changed it, and the best callback contact. Every question the Phones team does not need to ask again saves the client a call. If you notice a how-to or setting request that this guide does not cover, add it to the team's list so the Phones team can include it in the next training.

Each entry gives the query in the client's words, the fixes that worked, and who should handle it.

Desk phones offline, No Account, frozen or faulty

Most "phone not working" reports come down to one of three things: the phone lost its registration, it has no network (cable, port, Wi-Fi or ISP), or its firmware is out of date. Truly defective units are a small share, mostly phones that will not power on.

Desk phone shows "No Account" or "Account not registered"

Usually the phone lost its registration after an internet or power blip, or it is online in GDMS but not registered to its SIP account.

  • Power cycled the phone (unplugged power and LAN for about 30 seconds) or rebooted it from GDMS, and it re-registered (411253, 403011, 396794, 389738)

  • When the phone was online in GDMS but unregistered: manually provisioned it, or re-pushed the configuration in GDMS and rebooted (412904, 411655, 407188, 391544)

  • Phone was pointing at the wrong server version (V2, V3 or V4) or had no provisioning URL: added the correct config file or provisioning URL and the phone registered (403483, 390700, 379775, 368519)

  • Phone's SIP profile did not match the rest of the office: changed it to the same PJSIP profile the other phones use (414429, 385376)

  • Wrong MAC address on the extension, or a label change broke the device link: corrected the MAC or label and refreshed the configuration (388794, 395709)

  • Keeps coming back every few days: upgraded firmware on all phones and scheduled a daily after-hours automatic reboot (391647, 371103)

  • Every phone in the office at once: checked for an Adit outage or an internet outage at the practice before troubleshooting individual phones (399893, 383465, 400849). In one case service had been suspended and came back once Billing confirmed payment (407803).

Calls also show a phone can display "No Account" for a few minutes during a firmware upgrade; that is expected and clears when the upgrade finishes.

Who handles it: T1 first: one phone or all, known outage, power cycle, cable or Wi-Fi. Then the Phones team for GDMS reprovisioning, server or PJSIP config fixes and MAC corrections.

Phone not ringing, showing an X icon, or needing several reboots a day (old firmware)

Older firmware causes missed rings, registration drops, freezing and calls that keep ringing after they are answered.

  • Upgraded the phones to the approved firmware (1.0.13.127) in GDMS and they registered and started ringing (399628, 399581, 391956, 407539)

  • X icon on every corded phone: rebooted and upgraded the firmware (406025)

  • Phones needed several reboots a day: upgraded the firmware, which fixed the connection drops, and set a daily scheduled reboot (393397, 397969)

  • Phones kept ringing after the call was answered: pushed a firmware upgrade (401125)

Who handles it: Phones team.

Phone is dead or defective and needs a replacement

Mostly phones that will not power on, or that stay stuck on a boot screen after every reset.

  • Confirmed the fault and shipped a replacement under warranty (391263, 386075, 388453, 404213)

  • Set up the replacement: Asset Management added it to the account, then the Phones team reprovisioned it, upgraded firmware and added the extension to the call flow (406729, 392459, 412420, 401493)

  • Out-of-warranty phone that would not power on: gave the client replacement pricing (387371)

  • Client asked for return labels for old phones: phones past the 90-day return period do not need to be returned (414440, 414266)

Who handles it: T1 first: power cycle, swap the cable and port, try another outlet. Then the Phones team for warranty checks, replacements and setup.

Phone is frozen, stuck on "Initializing", or the buttons do not respond

  • Power cycled the phone by unplugging power and LAN for about 30 seconds (385436, 385246, 405190)

  • Still freezing: factory reset and reconfigured it (406732, 412199, 409389)

  • Kept going blank or restarting during calls: reprovisioned and rebooted (412312)

  • Menu key or keypad did nothing: the Menu key was set to Locked and was set to Unlocked in the phone settings (405318, 387946)

  • Stuck on "Network Initializing" even after resets: replaced as defective (391097)

Who handles it: T1 first: guide the power cycle. Then the Phones team for the factory reset, config and key settings.

Phone shows "No LAN Cable", "Network Down", offline, or IP 0.0.0.0

The cause is almost always physical: the cable, the port, the switch or the practice internet.

  • Cable was in the PC port on the back of the phone: moved it to the LAN port (410684, 405562, 413854)

  • Reseated or replugged the Ethernet cable (409619, 403898, 389674, 388540)

  • Faulty wall jack or switch port: moved the phone to a known-good port (414120, 407904, 409447, 405057)

  • Faulty cable: client replaced it. One phone was proven fine by connecting it to a mobile hotspot (384342, 410579, 383652)

  • Ethernet not passing internet: connected the phone to Wi-Fi as a workaround (393678, 402680)

  • ISP outage or new ISP: waited for service to return, and rebooted all phones after the switch (389744, 358934, 391988)

  • Practice firewall blocking phones: the client's IT whitelisted Adit's domains, then the phones were reconfigured (391556, 383558)

Who handles it: T1 first: LAN port, another port and cable, power cycle, ask about internet outages. Then the Phones team for whitelisting and reprovisioning.

  • Choppy audio on Wi-Fi: advised moving the phone to Ethernet (411211, 401543)

Who handles it: T1 for reconnecting on the phone. Phones team for web GUI or GDMS changes.

Phone will not power on

Who handles it: T1 first: PoE switch, outlet and adapter. Then the Phones team to ship parts or a replacement.

Cordless phones and base stations

Most fixes are one of four steps: re-subscribe the handset, bring the base back online, turn off DND, or update the base firmware.

Handset says "Not Subscribed", "Out of Range" or "Searching for base"

The handset has lost its pairing with the base.

  • Re-subscribed the handset to the base: on the handset, Menu, Registration, Subscribe (414859, 414451, 414687, 405398)

  • Would not re-subscribe: factory reset the handset (Menu, Settings, System Settings, PIN 0000), then subscribed it again (413394, 410202, 402532, 402166)

  • Handset asked for a PIN on reboot: factory reset and subscribed it (414385)

  • Handset was paired to the wrong base: reset it and subscribed it to the correct base (410904, 385504)

  • Stuck searching even after re-pairing: upgraded the base to the official firmware, reset base and handset, and paired again (397360, 401082)

Calls note that pulling and reinserting the handset battery sometimes lets a stuck subscription go through.

Who handles it: T1 first: re-subscribe and handset factory reset. Then the Phones team for base firmware and base resets.

All cordless phones are down: base offline, no lights or orange light

When every handset shows "Out of Range" at once, the base has lost power or network.

  • Had the client unplug and reconnect the base's power and Ethernet, and it came back online (413438, 412704, 410188, 405360)

  • "Out of Range" on every handset was the base being unpowered: reconnected power (403539, 399383)

  • Bad cable or port: swapped the LAN cable or moved the base to another port (406112, 389643, 387361)

  • Base on unstable internet or a separate network: moved it to a stable connection on the same network as the other phones (394711, 400804)

  • Base would not stay powered and was out of warranty: power port likely faulty, client advised to repair it (394708)

Who handles it: T1 first: power and Ethernet on the base, another port and cable. Then the Phones team if handsets still do not register.

Dial tone but cannot call out: "Call Release" error, 520 or 502, or "Account Disabled"

  • Call release errors with drops on a healthy base: PingPlotter showed heavy packet loss, client referred to their ISP (414606)

Who handles it: Phones team.
